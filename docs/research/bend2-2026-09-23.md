# Bend 2 for openDaisugi

Date: 2026-09-23. Method: a multi-agent web search. Each claim was checked by three separate votes, and a claim stayed only if at least two votes kept it. Every claim below cites its sources.

## Question

Bend2 by Victor Taelin (HigherOrderCO) evaluated as a tool for openDaisugi, a Python runtime-assurance gate for AI coding agents that checks each proposed action against an envelope with a Z3/SMT verifier before it runs (fail-closed), and distills repeated plans into reusable "pathways". Research: (1) What Bend2 is in 2026 exactly — language design (dependent types? proof checking? relation to Kind, HVM2/HVM3/HVM4, interaction combinators), current release state, licence, maturity, backends (CPU/GPU/C/JS), tooling, community size; distinguish Bend (v1) from Bend2 and cite dates; (2) Taelin's stated claims about Bend2 for AI/program synthesis (e.g. SupGen, superposition-based program search, "AI-assisted proof"), and what has been independently demonstrated vs only claimed; (3) whether Bend2's type system can serve as a verifier or proof checker for small specifications like shell-command policy predicates, compared to Z3/SMT, Lean 4, Dafny; performance, trust base, and soundness status; (4) concrete fit: could openDaisugi use Bend2 for verification, for pathway/program synthesis, or as a compile target — or should it not, and why. Cite source URLs for every claim and flag hype vs evidence.

## Summary

Bend 2 is a new dependently typed language from Victor Taelin, publicly launched on 2026-09-17. It is a clean break from Bend 1: Bend 1 programs and HVM do not carry over. It is Apache-2.0 and is marketed as a language that "blocks AI mistakes via proof". It is very young. The README says "EXPECT BUGS", and the compiler is "99% AI-written" and not fully audited. The project shipped 19 patch releases in six days (2.0.8 to 2.0.26, Sep 18-23), and several of them changed checker semantics or fixed soundness holes where `Empty` could be proved. Proofs are explicit terms, with no tactics and no proof search. SupGen/NeoGen, the superposition-based program search, is the headline AI-synthesis idea. Its claims ("beats all published alternatives", an O(n log n) sort found, HVM4 as a proof verifier "OOMs faster than Lean") are only Taelin's own reports, with no independent benchmark, and the evidence shows no SupGen proof search in the shipped Bend 2. For openDaisugi, Bend 2 should not be the fail-closed verifier or a compile target. The production precedent for policy verification is SMT (Cedar Analysis with cvc5), with a proof assistant (Lean) that certifies the encoder. That supports keeping Z3 and, if more assurance is wanted, proving the predicate-to-SMT encoding in Lean. Bend 2/SupGen is at most something to watch for pathway synthesis research.

## Findings

### 1. Bend 2 is a new, separate language, publicly launched on 2026-09-17. It is not an upgrade of Bend 1. Its licence is Apache-2.0. It presents itself as 'a fast language that blocks AI mistakes via proof', and it claims GPU execution. The human writes the spec (LAWS.bend) and the AI writes the proof.

Confidence: high. Vote: 3-0 (merged claims 0, 4, 5).

Evidence: Launch post created Thu Sep 17 20:20:07 UTC 2026: 'Bend 2 is here! It is a new programming language that blocks AI mistakes via *proof checking*'. README: 'With proofs, we can mechanically verify the AI implemented our prompts correctly' and 'Bend 2 is a new language. Bend 1 programs and HVM do not carry over.' The HigherOrderCO/Bend2 repo was created 2026-09-16. This is self-description and marketing, not independent proof. The same post compares the method to AI labs solving 'Navier-Stokes', which is hype.

### 2. Bend 2 is immature and its checker semantics are still changing. The project admits this, and the release history shows it: in patch releases, soundness bugs let a closed proof of `Empty` be written, and breaking type-checker changes shipped.

Confidence: high. Vote: 3-0 (merged claims 1, 2, 3).

Evidence: README: 'BEND IS YOUNG. EXPECT BUGS AND REPORT THEM' and 'The compiler (not kernel) is 99% AI-written and has not been fully audited yet.' GitHub API: 19 releases, v2.0.8 to v2.0.26, between 2026-09-18 and 2026-09-23, 8 of them on Sep 19 alone. v2.0.17 made a breaking change (operators take their type only from the enclosing `( .. : T)`) and a template redesign 'so the checker and the compiled binary cannot mean different things'. It also fixed a nat-literal pattern that 'was a closed proof of `Empty`' (#852). v2.0.21 (#902) and v2.0.25 (#941) fixed more ways to inhabit `Empty`. v2.0.8 fixed a hub-client regression from 2.0.6. A proof checker that accepted proofs of False this week cannot be the trusted base of a fail-closed gate.

Sources:
- https://github.com/bendlang/bend
- https://github.com/HigherOrderCO/Bend/releases

### 3. Bend 2 has dependent types with proofs as explicit terms. It has no tactic language and no proof search. SupGen, the superposition search, was not integrated with dependent types before launch, and the shipped docs do not mention it. Without it, the author said AI proof filling was too slow to be a viable product.

Confidence: medium. Vote: 3-0 (merged claims 9, 10).

Evidence: Taelin, about 2026-03-05 (pre-launch): Bend2-SupGen integration is 'really hard to do, due to Bend's dependent types. currently, SupGen is still reliable for simple types... we're talking about whole nights to make a small refactor.' The same post lists launch blockers with estimates: HVM4 AOT compiler 5-10 days, HVM4 GPU runtime about 15 days, SupGen integration 5-10 days. bend2.dev (updated 2026-09-20): 'The current implementation requires explicit proof terms, with no tactic language or proof search.' README: 'Bend has no tactics or proof search.' The March tweet was read through a search-index snippet, because x.com returned HTTP 402.

Sources:
- https://x.com/VictorTaelin/status/2029567059881857081
- https://bend2.dev/notes/what-is-bend2/
- https://github.com/bendlang/bend

### 4. Bend 1 (on HVM2, launched about late 2024) had serious practical limits and was abandoned. HVM2 was dropped for HVM3, a Haskell-led prototype, and HVM4, a C runtime. The author says HVM4 compiles Interaction Calculus to zero-overhead machine code, but gives no public benchmark.

Confidence: high. Vote: 3-0 (merged claims 11, 13).

Evidence: Nov 2025: Bend1 needed a 'full RTX 4090 to beat 1-core OCaml / JavaScript', had 'int24, no IO, no packages', and the author decided to 'throw HVM2 away'. The author admits this against his own interest, so the history is credible. The zero-overhead compilation claim ('including functions with superpositions') is only the author's. The HVM4 README lists bench files but publishes no results and warns 'you're here before launch. Use at your own risk.' A later post mentions HVM5, so the runtime layer is still changing.

Sources:
- https://x.com/VictorTaelin/status/1985423273304473788
- https://x.com/VictorTaelin/status/1985320306001477783
- https://github.com/HigherOrderCO/HVM4

### 5. SupGen/NeoGen, the superposition-based program search on HVM, is the main AI-synthesis claim. Every capability claim about it is self-reported and not independently checked. The search is still exponential in the size of the space, and the claimed gain is a large constant factor.

Confidence: medium. Vote: 3-0 (merged claims 6, 8, 12).

Evidence: Taelin: SupGen 'beats all published alternatives in efficiency and capabilities... capable of generating code based on examples and specs'. NeoGen: 'It is still exponential, but with a constant speedup large enough to find certain programs that were previously intractable, like a O(n*log(n)) sort' (Peano sort at index 5,723,095, found in 2 s). Sep 2025: 'SupGen is now built-in' to HVM4. There is no peer-reviewed paper, no third-party benchmark and no replication. The quotes are verified (3-0), so the author did say these things. That the claims are true is unverified, so treat them as hype until someone reproduces them.

### 6. The claim that HVM/Bend could serve as a fast, parallel proof verifier is an aspiration with no published soundness argument, trust-base description or benchmark.

Confidence: medium. Vote: 3-0 (merged claim 7).

Evidence: Sep 2025: 'we finally have a native type system, running directly on interaction nets, meaning HVM will be usable as a fast, parallel proof verifier. I'd not be surprised if it ends up being OOMs faster than Lean.' The 'OOMs faster than Lean' part is a guess, and a separate claim built on it was refuted 1-2. Verifier notes say the Lean formalization and the bend.ts implementation do not yet match. That note came from secondary sources, not from a direct check.

### 7. The production precedent for verifying authorization-policy predicates, the same kind of problem as openDaisugi's command policies, is SMT solving plus proof-assistant certification of the encoder. It is not a dependently typed checker language. AWS Cedar compiles policies to SMT, which cvc5 discharges over every possible request. The compiler is written and proved sound and complete in Lean, and the Rust code is tied to the Lean model by differential testing.

Confidence: high. Vote: 3-0 (merged claims 14, 15, 16).

Evidence: AWS (2025-06-16): Cedar Analysis 'translates your Cedar policies into mathematical formulas that capture every possible request the policy could allow', and SMT solvers such as cvc5 analyse them. The Symbolic Compiler is in Lean, so 'we [can] not only... implement the compiler but also... mathematically prove its correctness'. lean-lang.org: 'executable formal models in Lean alongside the production Rust implementation'; 'No new Cedar version is released unless its model, proofs, and differential tests are current.' Qualifications: cvc5 stays in the trusted base, the proofs cover the Lean model and not the Rust binary, and Cedar Analysis itself is newer than Cedar's production use.

Sources:
- https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/opensource/introducing-cedar-analysis-open-source-tools-for-verifying-authorization-policies/
- https://lean-lang.org/use-cases/cedar/
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01688

### 8. Fit for openDaisugi: do not use Bend 2 as the gate's verifier, do not make it a compile target, and do not depend on it for pathway synthesis now. Keep Z3 for per-action checks. If more assurance is wanted, follow the Cedar pattern: prove the predicate-to-SMT encoding in Lean 4. openDaisugi already has a Lean client in its multi-client differential harness. At most, watch SupGen as a research input for pathway synthesis over simple types.

Confidence: medium. Vote: inference from 3-0 claims.

Evidence: This is a synthesis of the findings above, not a single sourced claim. (1) As a verifier: soundness holes were fixed days ago, the compiler is unaudited and AI-written, and checker semantics change between patches, so it would widen the fail-closed trusted base against the gate's core law. It also gives no automation: proofs must be written as explicit terms, while Z3 decides policy predicates on its own. (2) As a synthesis engine: SupGen is unverified, exponential, and by the author's account was reliable only for simple types and not integrated with dependent types at launch. There is no Python API or embedding story. (3) As a compile target: no stable semantics, a new package hub, no evidence of Python interop, and a GPU runtime that the author said as late as March 2026 did not yet exist.

## Caveats

Time-sensitivity is extreme. Bend 2 launched six days before this report (2026-09-23) and ships several releases a day, so maturity findings may go stale within weeks. Most claims about Taelin's plans come from X posts that were read through search-index snippets, because x.com returned HTTP 402. The quotes were checked, but not always against the full thread. Two repos appear (bendlang/bend for the README, HigherOrderCO/Bend for releases). They seem to be the same project, but that was not confirmed. Several useful details come from verifier notes, not from voted claims: the Apache-2.0 licence, the '99% AI-written compiler' line, the Lean formalization not matching bend.ts, and the Hacker News critique that laws can be sidestepped by redefining subprograms. Weight them less. Refuted claims are left out on purpose: a LAWS.bend 'guarantee' framing (0-3), NeoGen being integrated into Bend2 (0-3), planned-feature lists and launch timelines from Nov 2025 (0-3), the 'little outside community' claim (0-3), and Lean's small-kernel trust base as a comparison point (1-2). The recommendation is an inference for openDaisugi, not a claim that any source states. The performance claims (GPU speed, OOMs faster than Lean, zero-overhead compilation) have no independent benchmark. The GPU and SupGen state at launch was not checked directly.

## Openquestions

- Does the shipped Bend 2 (2.0.26) include a GPU backend and any SupGen-based hole filling, or only CPU execution with explicit proofs? The launch post says 'runs on GPUs', but the March 2026 blockers list said there was no GPU runtime.
- What exactly is Bend 2's trusted kernel: its size, whether it is separate from the unaudited compiler, and whether any formal soundness proof or independent audit exists or is planned?
- Has anyone outside HigherOrderCO reproduced a SupGen/NeoGen result or compared it against published program-synthesis baselines (e.g. SyGuS, Myth, Synquid)?
- For openDaisugi, would a Lean 4 proof that the predicate-to-SMT-LIB2 encoder is correct (the Cedar pattern) be worth the cost, given the existing Lean client in the differential harness?
