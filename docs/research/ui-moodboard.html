A mood board · recreations, not screenshots

Tools that earn their keep — and the ones that don't

The theory this board studies: a great tool is either usable by an infant (one obvious affordance, near-zero learning) or an expert instrument (minimal energy, maximum skill — a high-throughput channel between a trained hand and the tool). The mediocre middle is where tools go to die. The one good middle is scaffolding — a ramp that carries a novice into the expert instrument, the way Bloomberg and vim's ghosts do. Companion report: tool-ui-design-bimodal.md.

Car HMI: buttons vs touchscreen

automotive
badsimple

On a track at 110 km/h: physical buttons did four tasks in ~10 s; a touch-everything car took 44.6 s.

Lesson: proven ~4×. The "put it all on one screen" middle is slower and more dangerous than a knob you can feel.

Hawaii false missile alert (2018)

emergency
bad

A dropdown put the real "ballistic missile inbound" one line from the drill. One misclick, no confirmation, a whole state panicked.
