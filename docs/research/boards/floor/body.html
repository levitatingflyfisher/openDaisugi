A floor you can talk to.

coppice runs the panes. The gate decides what is allowed. You say what you want in plain words. The floor tells you when it needs you, and stays quiet the rest of the time.

part two

What the survivors share

Five patterns that came through three-vote verification. They are the brief for every mockup below.

One roster, sorted by whether you are needed.Needs you on top. Working under it. Done at the bottom. The row shows the question, not the log.
The unit of work sits above the pane.A task has a label, a worktree, and one or more panes. Panes come and go. The task stays.
Counts and jumps, never interrupts.A number in the tab title. One key to the next thing that needs you. No modal ever steals the screen.
New work starts in plain words.An input line is the front door. Say what you want. The floor spawns, waits, and reports.
Three to five in hand. Any number live.People steer a few at a time and let the rest run. Blast off sixteen, focus on two, move on. The floor is what makes the other fourteen safe to ignore.
the whole vocabulary

Six keys. Three you already know.

No prefix. No chords to learn. Everything else you type is words to the floor. The mouse works too, on every row, tab, and tile.

ctrl t
Talk. Brings the floor and its prompt from anywhere. New work starts here.
shift tab
Next one that needs you. Cycles in attention order, not screen order.
ctrl w
Close what you are looking at. The task keeps its worktree.
space
Peek. Shows the last lines and the ask without leaving the roster.
enter
Go in. Attach to the pane. Your keys belong to the harness now.
esc
Zoom out. Peek closes. Roster returns to the line.

command line · cold start

Type the name. Then talk.

The first screen is a sentence and a prompt. No dashboard, no config, no empty grid. One line per task after you speak, with the harness and the worktree, because those are the two facts you will need next.

  • Works before it is configured. Harnesses found on PATH are the harnesses.
  • Output is plain text on stdout. Pipe it and it stays readable.
  • The floor never asks a question here that it can answer itself.
tui · the roster

Sorted by whether you are needed.

This is the attention queue. It is also the only list. Rows carry the question, not the last log line. The tab title and every footer carry the count, so you can leave and still know.

  • The unknown state is not allowed to exist on this screen. If the floor cannot tell, it says quiet and shows how long.
  • Sixteen live panes is fine. When more than a few need you at once, the header says so in words, and the queue is the order to take them in.
  • The prompt line never leaves the bottom.
  • In a wide terminal the selected pane shows live on the right, the lazygit shape. Rows are clickable. The keys are the fast path, not the only path.

tui · peek

Look without going in.

Space opens the last lines and the ask. The gate's verdict is its own line, in its own colour, with the rule that fired. You answer in words or with one key. The pane never sees your keystrokes.

  • The verdict comes from the envelope, not from the model. The model only proposed.
  • Deny is one key. Allow is graded by blast radius. See three tiers below.
  • Esc returns you exactly where you were in the roster.
tui · attached

One thin line of chrome.

Inside a pane the harness owns the screen. coppice keeps one footer line: where you are, how many need you, and the one key that leaves. Nothing else. The count is the same number that sits in the tab title.

  • No status bar with clocks, load, or battery. Your terminal already has one.
  • The footer turns amber when someone new needs you. It does not pop anything.
  • shift-tab leaves for the next one that needs you. ctrl-t leaves for the prompt.

tui · the foreman

A normal pane, promoted.

The thing you talk to is a frontier model in your harness of choice. It is promoted by one page: the coppice commands it already has in its shell, no tool schema. It spawns, waits, prompts, and reads. It cannot allow. The gate is outside it.

  • Every tool call it makes is printed in dim, one line each. No hidden hands.
  • It plans in words and asks once when the plan needs a person.
  • Not a small local model. Judging when to escalate is the whole job.
  • Many hands: every allow and deny names who gave it, and a row says who else is looking. A name gives no right.
tui · three zoom levels

The line, the roster, the pane.

Zoom in with Enter, zoom out with Esc. The same count travels through all three levels, so you always know what you are not looking at.

  • The line is what the tab title, the shell prompt, and the phone banner show.
  • Nothing lives between these three. No dashboards of dashboards.

laptop · the floor page

Three tiles, one rail, one prompt.

The web view is the same shape as the terminal: the roster, plus as many live panes as the width allows. Here that is three. A minimap of the tree sits at the bottom of the rail, one dot per node, colour is state, click to jump. Three tiles are the ones in your hands. The rail holds the other thirteen, sorted by who needs you, so the count of live panes never has to be small. Swapping a tile in is a click on the rail. The ask sits in an amber bar on the tile, never in a dialog.

phone · the attention queue, the quiet screen, the lock screen

Thumb sized. Deny is the big one.

The phone shows the same queue with two facts per ask: what it wants and what the gate said. Deny is always one tap. The quiet screen is a real screen, not an empty list, because most of the time the honest answer is that nothing needs you. The lock screen card carries the same two facts and the same two buttons.

every surface · three tiers of allow

The guard matches the blast radius.

Confirmation boxes wear out. A person answers the same box the same way after the fifth time. So coppice does not use one box. It uses three tiers, and each looks different.

  • Inside the envelope. Nothing is asked. It runs. The roster shows a dim line so you can see it happened.
  • Outside, but undoable. One key or one tap. The rule that fired is on the same line.
  • Outside and permanent. Type the pane's name. Deny is still one key.

tui · tasks and trees

The task is the unit. The pane is a hand.

A task has a name, a worktree, and however many panes it needs. Closing a pane does not lose the branch. This is the git tree view the roster folds away by default, one key from the roster.

  • Ahead and behind counts come from git, not from the model's memory.
  • A task's state is the worst state of its panes. Needs you beats working.
  • Worktrees live beside the repo on real disk, never in a RAM tmp.

plumbing · one socket, many faces

Every screen above is a client. So is yours.

The server does not know which porcelain is looking. The TUI, the floor page, the phone, and the foreman all speak the same nine verbs over the same socket and listen to the same notification stream. The gate sits beside the server, not inside any client, so no porcelain can talk its way past it.

# ~/.config/coppice/coppice.toml · the whole config
[harness.claude]
command = "claude"

[harness.pi-llama]

[harness.sprig]
command = "sprig"

# no plugins key: every shipped view, and notify-ntfy
plugins = ["tree", "minimap", "kanban", "colony", "shift-log",
           "herdr-grid", "inbox", "notify-ntfy", "merge-on-green"]
# ~/.config/coppice/plugins/merge-on-green/manifest.json
{
  "id": "merge-on-green",
  "kind": "policy",
  "listens": ["state"],
  "needs": ["task.list", "agent.prompt", "floor.note"],
  "run": "merge-on-green.py"
}

# a view plugin is the same shape with "kind": "view"
# and a "page": "index.html" served at /plugins/kanban/.
# a view runs sandboxed and holds no token. the floor page posts it the data.
# it may post back three things: open a pane, set the selection, watch nine panes.
# the coppice. namespace is reserved. everything else is yours.
# a policy can prompt and wait. it can never allow.
# every view ships on. one rule: every mark means a state the socket reports.

Seven views ship, and each one is on by default. Each opens on the floor page at #/view/<id>, and the rail carries a button for each. The source of each is one directory: tree the task tree as text and as a graph · minimap the tree shrunk to dots at the foot of the rail · kanban · colony · shift-log · herdr-grid a roster and a live picture of up to nine panes · inbox the tasks with a worktree, the ones that need you first, ! to jump.

shift-log · a view plugin, on by defaultRows are panes, cells are five minutes, colour is work. Amber is an ask, red is a deny. It answers one question honestly: where did the hour go. The web server keeps the last two hours of state events, so a reload keeps them. A span the server did not see, while its subscription was down or before it started, draws as unknown and never as a quiet one.
