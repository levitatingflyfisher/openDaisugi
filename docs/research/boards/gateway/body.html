A meter, then a chooser.

A local proxy any harness points at through its base URL. It routes an easy turn to a cheap model, never a hard one, meters what that saved from real counters, and fails open so you lose savings, never the turn.

gateway
The meterSpeaks the Anthropic wire and the OpenAI wire. Journals every turn's cache reads, cache writes, fresh and output tokens.
switchyard
The chooserNVIDIA's trained router, upstream of the meter. It picks the model. We count. Plan 09.

part two

What holds

From the token-saving landscape of August 2026 and the cockpit research.

Caching is the lever. Routing is second.Cache reads cost a tenth. Real coding-agent steps are about 96 percent cache reads. Keep the prefix byte-stable before you route anything.
Meter from provider counters.Tokens saved is a fold over usage fields on every response. Never an estimate, never a vendor's peak claim.
The honest ceiling is about three times.Blended, across a real session. Not a hundred. Say so on the status screen.
The human pause is the cost cliff.Misses cluster after gaps over five minutes. A gate that parks an agent is that pause. Show time since last request and keep parked sessions warm.
Fail open here, fail closed at the gate.A router that cannot route passes the turn through untouched. The two layers have opposite failure rules on purpose.

should be

The chooser upstream, the box on the tailnet, the cache on the row.

  • Switchyard upstream. The gateway stays the meter. Switchyard chooses. One flag composes them. Plan 09.
  • Any self-hosted box. One command probes a host on the tailnet, qualifies its model, and wires it in as a tier. Plan 08.
  • Cache state on the roster. Time since last request and expected warm or cold, per pane, in the floor's row. A miss on a pane that should have hit is an alert.
